# Chapter 3: Logic Core Reconstruction

> **Algorithm Goal:** Abandon the concept of deterministic orbits, redefine the "state" of microscopic particles using probability density functions, and understand the fundamental limits of information extraction.

**Corresponding topics: wave-particle duality, uncertainty relations, statistical interpretation of the wave function**

---

The Bohr model was a success, at least for hydrogen. But at its core it was a classical model patched with quantization: the electron still moved along a definite orbit, only the orbits had been discretized.

What this chapter undertakes is far more radical: we will demolish the very concept of "orbit" itself.

A microscopic particle is neither a classical "ball" nor a classical "wave." It inherits properties from both, yet in its behavior it transcends both. Its state is no longer described by a definite position and velocity, but by a **probability density function**. And there is an unbreakable limit on how much information you can extract from that function: the uncertainty relation.

This is not a limitation of measurement technology. It is a design specification of the universe's underlying architecture.
