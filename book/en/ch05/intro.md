# Chapter 5: Metadata and Perturbations

> **Algorithm Goal:** Discover the intrinsic degree of freedom of the electron (spin), understand how it couples with orbital motion, and how an external magnetic field causes energy level splitting.

**Corresponding topics: Stern-Gerlach experiment, electron spin, Zeeman effect, alkali metal doublets**

---

Up through Chapter 4, we possessed a complete toolkit for describing the hydrogen atom. The Schrodinger equation yielded all energy levels and wave functions, and the quantum numbers $(n, l, m)$ fully defined the orbital state of the electron.

But experiment disagreed.

High-resolution spectroscopy revealed that many "single" spectral lines are actually composed of two or more extremely closely spaced lines, the **fine structure**. The principal series of alkali metals (such as sodium's D line) also appear as doublets. The Stern-Gerlach experiment went even further, directly showing that a beam of silver atoms splits into exactly two beams in an inhomogeneous magnetic field, rather than the $2l+1$ beams one might expect.

These phenomena cannot be explained by the three quantum numbers $(n, l, m)$ alone. The answer is that the electron possesses a hidden degree of freedom: **spin**.
